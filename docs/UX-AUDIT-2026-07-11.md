## Part 1 — Three Demo Simulations (FS AI Risk Manager)

### Demo 1 · Onboarding a new agentic capability into the model‑risk register
**Trigger.** The Markets division proposes an **agentic research copilot** that autonomously pulls market data, calls internal pricing/booking tools, and drafts trade rationales. As second line, I must translate this capability into a **control set** for the SR 11‑7 model‑risk inventory before it clears governance.

**Path through the tool.**
1. **Overview** → scan the 10 ASI risk cards; the copilot's autonomy + tool access flags four as in‑scope: **ASI01 Agent Goal Hijack (Critical)**, **ASI02 Tool Misuse**, **ASI03 Identity & Privilege Abuse (Critical)**, **ASI06 Memory & Context Poisoning**.
2. **Evidence Explorer → ASI02** → the **800‑53 column** returns AC‑3/AC‑6 (access enforcement, least privilege), SI‑10 (input validation), AU‑2 (audit events); the **AI‑RMF column** returns MAP‑4 (third‑party risk) and MEASURE‑2 (trustworthiness eval); each family card carries a rationale and an **"Editorial"** validation badge. The evidence strip cites the **Amazon Q secrets‑via‑DNS‑exfiltration** incident (embracethered) — a live example of exactly this tool‑misuse class.
3. **800‑53 Heatmap** → confirm coverage density for the four risks; note AC/SI/AU are "hot," and that MP/PE are unmapped (disclosed in the scope explainer — not a gap in *my* scope).
4. **Scenario Simulator** → generate a fact pattern, toggle **`has_agent_limits` = off** → AC/SC families flip **Broken**, visually demonstrating the residual exposure if action‑scoping isn't enforced — useful ammunition for a control requirement.
5. **Footer → Download CSV** → seed the register entry.

**Value delivered.** In ~10 minutes I moved from "new capability" to a defensible, evidence‑backed candidate control set spanning both the technical (800‑53) and governance (AI‑RMF) frameworks — work that otherwise takes a half‑day of manual OWASP/NIST cross‑referencing.
**Where it stops short.** I still hand‑assemble the control set in my head and **re‑key it into Archer**; the CSV is the whole dataset, not my scoped selection; nothing persists the analysis for the next review cycle.

### Demo 2 · Quarterly AI Risk Committee / board reporting
**Trigger.** The AI Risk Committee wants a one‑slide view of the firm's **agentic‑AI threat posture and control coverage**, tied to our stated NIST AI RMF adoption.

**Path.** Hero **stat cards** (10 risks, **4 Critical**) → **800‑53 Heatmap** for the coverage‑density narrative → **AI‑RMF Heatmap** to show coverage across **GOVERN / MAP / MEASURE / MANAGE** (which maps cleanly to the committee's AI‑RMF story) → **Provenance & Limitations** panel to answer "how current and how authoritative is this?" (pinned framework editions + disclosed editorial limits) → screenshot into the deck.

**Value delivered.** The two heatmaps + provenance panel give a credible, framework‑aligned posture snapshot with an honesty story a CRO can stand behind.
**Where it stops short.** Screenshots are **dark‑only** (off‑brand in a governance deck); the heatmap shows *data*, not a **ranked** "here's where we're most exposed" story; there is no board/print view, so I rebuild the whole thing in PowerPoint.

### Demo 3 · Regulatory exam / internal‑audit response (the killer use case)
**Trigger.** An examiner asks: *"Demonstrate how you mitigate unauthorized or rogue agent behavior in your autonomous systems."*

**Path.** **Evidence Explorer → ASI10 Rogue Agents** (and **ASI01 Goal Hijack**) → mapped controls with framework‑scoped hover tooltips — **AC‑6** (least privilege), **AU‑2** (audit), **SI‑4** (system monitoring), **IR** (circuit breakers/kill‑switch) — each with a rationale, and cited real‑world evidence (impersonated‑observer‑agent fraud; reward‑hacking → Anthropic research) with **external links and CVEs**. The **"Editorial" badges** honestly signal these are editorial mappings, not third‑party‑attested — which is itself the right disclosure to make to an examiner.

**Value delivered.** This is the product's strongest moment: I can answer the examiner with **specific control citations + source‑verified justification + provenance**, in minutes — the difference between an assertion and a citation.
**Where it stops short.** I cannot export a **scoped examiner‑response brief** (control set + rationale + cited evidence + provenance stamp) — I screenshot and reassemble; and the validation status shown is the *vendor's*, with no way to record my institution's own reviewer/owner/concurrence.

## Part 2 — Value‑Proposition Validation

**Stated value proposition (as built):** *"Compliance middleware — the Rosetta Stone that translates the OWASP Top‑10 agentic‑AI threats into the NIST control (800‑53 Rev 5) and governance (AI‑RMF) frameworks a regulated firm already reports against, with source‑verified evidence you can hand a regulator."*

**Verdict: the value proposition is real, differentiated, and validated for the "understand + cite" jobs — but currently delivered as a *reference atlas* rather than a *workflow‑of‑record*, which caps its enterprise value.**

**What genuinely validates it (the moat):**
- **The three‑way crosswalk itself** is the exact translation layer this persona lives in daily; "compliance middleware" is the right frame and there is no obvious peer doing all three frameworks at once.
- **Source‑verified evidence is unique.** 33 real, adversarially‑verified incidents with CVEs and external links (EchoLeak→CVE‑2025‑32711, AgentFlayer→Zenity, the Replit production‑DB deletion→The Register) turn an abstract mapping into examiner‑ready material. All three auditors independently named this the moat.
- **Exam‑grade transparency.** Editorial‑severity disclosure, a 3‑status validation model, pinned `UPSTREAM_VERSIONS` provenance, a monthly upstream‑drift watch, and a "Provenance & Limitations" panel are the intellectual honesty a second line and a regulator reward — and that most vendor GRC content fakes or omits.

**What limits it for this persona (the gaps that cap value):**
- **It stops at "read."** It completes **zero of the persona's five jobs end‑to‑end** — every one ends with "…now re‑assemble this by hand and re‑key it into the GRC tool." The atomic object is the *mapping cell*, not the *deliverable*.
- **It speaks OWASP+NIST, not the frameworks the bank reports against** — **SR 11‑7 / OCC 2011‑12, EU AI Act, ISO/IEC 42001, DORA**. The last‑mile translation the analyst actually needs is left to them.
- **The "wow" is buried.** The evidence — the single most persuasive asset — sits three decisions deep behind a filter pill; the hero has no "start here."
- **Not committee‑ready or shareable.** Dark‑only, whole‑dataset JSON/CSV, no per‑risk permalink/one‑pager/OG image, no board/print view, no mobile.

**Net:** a strong **wedge** (crosswalk + verified evidence + provenance) that earns credibility on contact, gated behind a reference‑tool information architecture. Converting the wedge into an enterprise tool of record is a last‑mile‑artifact problem, not a content problem — the hard part (trustworthy, cited data) is already done.

## Part 5 — Full Audit · Clarity/Trust Lens (OpenAI‑caliber)

**Verdict — 72/100.** *An unusually honest, well‑layered crosswalk that genuinely earns trust — but it's built for someone who already speaks 800‑53, and it stops one step short of the deliverable its user actually came to produce.*

**Gets right:** (1) Transparency is *structural, not cosmetic* — editorial disclaimer + 3‑status validation legend/counts + per‑family badges + provenance panel form an honesty posture most tools fake; for a user whose currency is defensibility this is the single best design decision. (2) Layered depth that respects attention (Overview → card → Explorer → tooltip is textbook progressive disclosure; deep‑linkable `?tab=`). (3) Real, typed, cited evidence — the fastest genuine "aha."

**Findings:** [High] the "so‑what‑for‑me" translation to SR 11‑7 / three‑lines / residual risk is missing (speaks OWASP+NIST, governance not at all). [High] control IDs are unexplained jargon gated behind an undiscoverable hover (render titles inline; click‑expand). [High] first‑run "aha" too slow — best moment is 3+ decisions deep (hero "See a worked example →"). [High] scattered honesty never resolves into an *aggregate* confidence posture (add a trust bar). [Medium] export serves an engineer not a committee (per‑risk PDF brief). [Medium] the 800‑53 heatmap overloads before it orients (default‑open explainer, in‑cell counts, adjacent legend). [Medium] the Simulator speaks control‑plane, not residual‑risk (relabel toggles/outputs). [Low] no terminology on‑ramp (primer + inline defs).

**Signature insight:** *Invert the object model from "explore a crosswalk" to "assemble a deliverable," and promote the receipts from footnote to headline.* The honesty layer is positioned defensively ("don't over‑trust us"); reframed, it **is** the value prop: "the only agentic‑AI crosswalk you can hand a regulator, because every claim carries its receipt." Rotate the product 90°: same content, organized around walking out with a provenance‑stamped brief.

**Do only five:** (1) per‑risk Export brief (PDF); (2) aggregate Evidence‑confidence bar; (3) inline control titles + "how to read this" primer; (4) hero "See a worked example →"; (5) governance‑translation line per risk.

(3) Purposeful density with pre‑empted objections (heatmap density→popover→drill; scope explainer pre‑answers "why isn't X here?").

**Findings:** [Critical] no control‑set selection model — there is no "cart" for the #1 job (add a persistent Control‑Set tray). [Critical] OSCAL is in the data package but invisible in the UI — the integration job is blocked (surface a scoped "Download OSCAL"). [High] export is a whole‑dataset dump, not a committee artifact (scoped printable memo). [High] no persistence — no named, re‑openable assessment (saved Assessments + JSON import/export; encode selection in URL). [High] no coverage‑scoring / gap register / institutional RACI overlay (Gap view + local status‑override/owner/RACI). [High] no bridge to SR 11‑7 / EU AI Act / ISO 42001 / DORA (editorial mapping columns + SR 11‑7 lifecycle tags). [Medium] no session audit trail. [Medium] density has an analyst register but no exec/print register (print stylesheet + Board‑view toggle).

**Signature insight:** *Flip the object model: the atomic unit should be a "defensible control set," not the crosswalk.* `{capability → selected controls → cited evidence → validation status → analyst annotation → provenance stamp}` — the crosswalk demotes to the palette. Your differentiator (verified evidence + provenance) creates enterprise value only when it travels attached to a decision; that single object closes four of five jobs and makes the tool the thing the register points *to*.

**Do only five:** (1) control‑set tray; (2) scoped exports incl. OSCAL + a printable committee memo; (3) saved Assessments; (4) coverage/gap view + status‑override/owner/RACI; (5) editorial ISO 42001 / EU AI Act / SR 11‑7 bridge.

## Bottom line

The product has already done the hard, credibility‑defining work — a correct, test‑verified three‑way crosswalk with **source‑verified evidence and exam‑grade provenance** (the moat all three auditors named). The gap between "credible reference" and "enterprise tool of record" for a financial‑services AI risk manager is a **last‑mile‑artifact and information‑architecture problem**, not a content problem: (1) let the user **assemble and export a defensible, cited control set / risk brief** (R1–R2, R5); (2) **lead with the evidence** and give the hero a spine (R3); (3) **speak the frameworks the bank reports against** (R4). Those three moves convert a strong wedge into a workflow the second line would return to every review cycle.
